Case study · Tidewater Freight

How Tidewater Freight cut gross churn 18% in 12 months.

A health score nobody trusted, rebuilt around four signals from their own renewal history, and three Playbooks that act on it.

The company

Tidewater Freight makes shipment visibility software for mid-size logistics companies. 600 employees, 1,400 customer accounts, a customer success team of 22 CSMs.

The problem

Churn arrived as a surprise. The team's health score was a CRM formula nobody trusted, so CSMs ignored it and worked from memory. Most at-risk accounts were spotted less than 30 days before renewal, which is too late to change anything.

What they did

Tidewater's CS Operations lead rebuilt the score in Harbor Desk Health Scores around four signals that their own renewal history showed mattered: weekly active users against seats, tracking-API errors, escalated tickets and executive sponsor changes. They back-tested it against eight quarters of renewals before turning it on, then launched three Playbooks: sponsor change, usage drop and pre-renewal review at 120 days.

The result

Gross churn fell 18% in the first 12 months. At-risk accounts now turn amber about 70 days earlier than before, and the sponsor change Playbook alone saved 31 accounts in its first year.

"We used to find out an account was at risk when the cancellation email arrived. Now the health score turns amber about 70 days earlier, and the playbook has already put it on my team's list."

Dana Whitlock, VP of Customer Success, Tidewater Freight
